What is migraine?

Migraine is a neurological condition that can cause recurring attacks. Head pain may be throbbing and moderate to severe, but symptoms vary; some people experience other symptoms as well.

◉

More than head pain

An attack may also involve nausea, vomiting, or sensitivity to light, sound, or smells.

⌁

Different for each person

Not everyone has the same symptoms or warning signs. Some people experience aura, such as visual changes or tingling.

♡

Support is available

A healthcare professional can assess recurring or severe headaches and discuss suitable treatment options.

Common symptoms

Symptoms can differ from one attack to another. These examples are educational and cannot confirm whether someone has migraine.

During an attack, someone may have:

  • Throbbing or pulsing head pain, sometimes on one side
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Sensitivity to light or sound
  • Pain that gets worse with routine movement
  • Tiredness or difficulty concentrating

Some people experience aura:

  • Flashing lights, zigzag lines, or other visual changes
  • Tingling or pins-and-needles sensations
  • Temporary difficulty speaking

New, sudden, or unusual neurological symptoms need urgent medical assessment rather than being assumed to be migraine.

Everyday steps that may help

These general habits may help some people manage migraine. They do not replace medical advice, and no single routine works for everyone.

☾

Keep a regular routine

Try to keep sleep and meal times reasonably consistent, and avoid skipping meals where possible.

⌁

Notice personal patterns

A private diary of timing, symptoms, sleep, meals, and possible triggers may help you discuss patterns with a clinician. Triggers vary by person.

☷

Rest and hydrate

During an attack, some people find a quiet, dark room, rest, and drinking fluids helpful.

About medicines and supplements Do not start, stop, or change medicines or supplements based on this website. Ask a qualified healthcare professional or pharmacist what is appropriate for your situation.

When to seek urgent medical help

Get emergency medical help if a headache is sudden and extremely severe, or occurs with new weakness, trouble speaking, confusion, loss of vision, a seizure, high fever with a stiff neck, or after a head injury. These can be signs of a serious condition.

Seek medical advice if headaches are new, worsening, unusually long, happening often, or interfering with everyday life. Use your local emergency number or emergency service when symptoms are urgent.

Do not wait for this website to respond to urgent symptoms.
